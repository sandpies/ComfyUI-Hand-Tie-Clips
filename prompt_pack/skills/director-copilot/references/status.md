# Which rules are proven

Every rule here came from a render that failed. Lean on the proven ones. For each piece, pick two
or three unproven ones the story can test, tell the user which, and move a rule up once the user
has watched the render and says it held (or rewrite it if it failed). Frame stats and audio levels
can only move a rule down.

## Proven (held in at least one render after the rule was written)

**Join modes and form**
- `hand_tie` one-take rap MV, every hop opening on one framing sentence (size, person, place tag)
  and the plate carrying the room: all four joins held at native-like motion (user-watched, 10-28).
- `hand_tie` + `master_audio_file` for a one-place, one-take MV: 12 hops x 15 s (2:51), lip sync
  held across all 11 joins.
- `hand_tie` join windows kept calm (hands resting on the armrests, the camera holding, the same
  framing sentence every hop): a 5-hop one-take rap MV held its framing and joins across three
  previs sections (an earlier MV with hands moving at the joins blurred at three of them).
- A music video where nobody sings on screen.
- Three `motion_context` continuations in a row in one scene; a 12-hop, 2.5-minute film of cuts,
  tracks, arcs and holds written from a scene map.

**Directing**
- The camera in the beat's prose, `camera` and `framing` left empty.
- `[Shot N]` cuts with timed action: 3-4 shots in a hop with lines; 7-8 in a no-line action hop,
  also with two character sheets on it.
- Every cut re-places everyone; a continuation's `[Shot 1]` continues the pin, no cut for 2 s.
- The same gap as a number in both shots of a cut; seats restated in each shot's own frame.
- An end mark on every run or walk; a leap with a landing ("lands in a crouch and stays crouched");
  a person starting a few steps into the frame and walking to the mark.
- Someone heard but not seen kept behind the camera; a reverse angle naming its background; an
  arrival through a door the plate shows; someone behind a lit shop window, the camera outside.
- Moves: hold, arc, rise, push in (rationed), track or back away ahead, tilt up, a fast zoom to a
  close, a drone high wide, a low mounted camera, a helmet POV, a drone alongside (the last four on
  subjects with no sheet); an orbit kept to half a turn, stopping on the face.
- A person on a flat solid colour background with no plate.
- A match cut on a small object; a daylight flashback in a night film with the look in the style
  line and the time of day in the beats.
- A look behind written as the turn ("looks up over her shoulder at the clock").
- One named extra who acts on cue.
- Simultaneous actions bound with "and / while / as / ;", sequence with "then".

**People and lines**
- Film eyelines: three-quarter toward the camera, eyes on the other person, in medium and wider.
- In close shots: the camera beside the listener, the gaze "passing well to the left of the
  camera"; a close closed with "alone in the frame ... nobody else is in the frame".
- The face that ends a hop shot as a close from beside the listener.
- Voiced rows on speaking hops, voiceless rows elsewhere.
- After the last line, the mouth closed and a sound named.
- Three speakers in a chosen order: each line its own shot, the speaker alone, every silent face
  closed, the first speaker's mouth free.
- A sting written on the reveal cut (lands about half a second early).
- Symmetric character designs hold identity across angles; a one-sided feature named with its
  side in `locked`, on a sheet that agrees.
- A watcher alone in the frame, far off the path of someone "already gone"; a phone dialled with
  the receiver down; a low score note across a quiet hop.

**Props and places**
- Props fixed in words inside one scene by colour, count and parts.
- A prop the plate lacks, counted and owned from its first shot.
- A key prop with its own picture, named by tag in every shot: one design across five hops.
- A small prop handed over on screen, each handover its own timed action.
- A picture inside the picture given its own medium; a mark "already circled ... stays exactly as
  it is", the hand beside it.
- A person seen only in a photo given a prop picture of that photo: the same face, no bleed; a
  person meeting her own photo.
- Plates shot from the shot's camera; one plate per shot in multi-shot hops; a small room on two
  plates at most.
- Stylised films: the medium named in every plate prompt, `establish` and `prompt_prefix`.
- Car interiors with no plate, kept medium or close and named in words; front seats placed by the
  frame from the dashboard.
- A vehicle with its own sheet, its tag on every hop, its plates made to match: one car model
  across seven angles.
- A high wide from above a moving vehicle with sheeted people, the plate shot from that height.

**Story**
- Three or four turns per minute; a tease held 2-3 s with `tail: hold`.

**Continuity**
- A restart that cuts on an action into a clearly new angle (low three-quarter from the person's
  front, the camera tracking with them to their next spot) read as a clean cut, where the same
  restart opening on a near-identical two-shot or a same-side over-the-shoulder read as a jump.
  Held twice.
- A spot phrase rewritten to the furniture's real orientation in the plate (naming the wall it
  runs along) kept the furniture in place; a phrase the room could not hold had moved it mid-room.
- Rerouting a walk to open floor the plate shows ended the clipping through furniture.
- Naming the garment's defining cut (neckline) in the outfit phrase fixed a close restart that had
  redrawn the garment plainer.
- An undress run as one unbroken shot (turn, zip, drop, then the cut): a version cutting
  mid-undress had replayed it; the one-shot version held (user-watched).
- Waist-up framing plus "the low glass table a full step in front of them" ended bare legs
  clipping through a glass table.
- An arousal state phrase in every nude shot: the version naming it only where touched drew him
  at rest (from the sheet) and made it appear on mention; the state-phrase version held.
- `sand_tie` on a fixed-camera adult take with no lines (five windows, three position changes):
  held as one shot (user: "mint").

- A lower-face skull half-mask singing a whole MV, the beats giving the jaw every word ("the jaw of
  the skull half-mask working with every word"): held, user-watched (11-13).
- A masked character masked from the first frame, the mask phrase naming the bare forehead, brows
  and eyes above it: held through 15 hops (11-13).

**Sitcom film** (one user-passed 10-hop pilot, 11-15: "done and good")
- An entrance written as one continuous take from the door to the mark, the camera pushing in with the
  walker, after a cut there had read as a jump.
- A vanish revealed by a move: a close on a watcher, the camera arcing round and drawing back until the
  empty spot is in frame, a soft chime, heads shaking.
- A whole exit in one take with everyone and every prop named from the first frame (the cup set down and
  named as left there in every later shot).
- Dialogue through a lower-face skull half-mask ("the jaw ... working with every word"), voiced row on
  speaking hops only.
- Five voiced characters across a film, at most three voices per hop, banter at about two words a second.
- Two plates per set (a view and its reverse), spots named by which end of the counter.

**Finish and retakes** (one user-passed MV, 11-14: "pass, fantastic")
- A black-and-white film rendered in colour, the noir look carried by hard practical light in the
  plates and beats, then graded to mono in the edit in one ffmpeg pass at the render's own bitrate,
  yuv420p. Colour refs and plates stay usable and every hop gets the identical grade.
- White dips (`transitions.py`, 0.6 s) on the cut into a flashback and the cut back out.
- A wordless humming choir kept as dark silhouettes against bright windows, faces in shadow, while
  the lead sings in the foreground.
- Retaking one hard-cut continuation and locking every hop after it (`locked: true`): the locked
  hops loaded from cache; the retake's last frames met the next locked hop with a small mismatch
  the user still read as coherent, because that join is a cut.

**Cut-every-bar anime rap MV** (one user-passed 15-hop, 2:42 render, 11-20: "mint ... magnifico",
everything held except the carriage hops listed under Failed)
- 4-7 `[Shot N]` cuts per hop over a master song with one singer and no `<d>` lines (73 shots in
  15 hops, 34-53 words a second, the outfit phrase in every shot): no truncation, shots held.
- Every new place as a hard-cut continuation (no anchor), the hop before ending on her face at a
  different size and angle each time: seven teleports held.
- Named pose hits that land on the downbeat and freeze, in place of "dances": no flailing arms.
- Single-clip moves inside a chain, with a sheet on the hop: eye macro, worm's-eye squat over the
  lens, axial cuts, whip pans landing dead still, fisheye close, a Dutch tilt ratcheting per beat,
  a top-down shot from its own plate, flat white and black void shots inside a plated hop, a knee
  slide, a crash zoom, a half-turn arc around a frozen pose, a leap with a crouch landing.
- Close shots written "alone in the frame, nobody else is in the frame", with a short place
  instead of the wide's full spot phrase.
- Finish (user: "job well done"): kinetic word flashes burned in post with an ASS file through
  ffmpeg libass, not subtitles. A few hero words per lyric moment, timed to word timestamps,
  placed opposite the face. Each scene gets a style from its light: neon glow on dark shots;
  a tape bar or flat yellow on bright ones (plain white text read as tacky). Mix a heavy
  condensed font with marker/glitch accents and a JP display face. Then a 3 s photosensitivity
  card, 0.2 s white dips on the hard-cut seams, and a 1 s fade to white at the end.

## Failed (tried on purpose; do not rely on it)

- A close shot carrying the wide's full spot phrase ("standing on the left roof three steps back
  from its inner edge above the gap"): a second, distant full-body copy stood in the background
  (11-20). In a close, keep a short place and write "alone in the frame".
- A swing round a pole, then "her sneakers braced together" while leaning out from it: the legs
  crossed and merged at the pole's base (11-20). Keep both feet planted apart and clear of the pole.
- A near-photographic plate (glossy floor, metallic highlights, bright even light) in an anime
  piece: the body came out realistic under the anime face for both hops on it (11-20). Plates in
  the piece's medium with line art and flat fills; name the medium on the person in those shots.

- A selective colour hold (ffmpeg `colorhold`) on candle flames in a mono grade: caught the flames
  weakly and leaked onto skin tones (11-14).

- A hop's end state in its own short last window ("At 00:15.800-00:17.000, @kyo stands with her lips
  closed in a cold smirk"): a hard cut there. Fold it into the window before (lint `end-state-window`).
- A plate's contents listed in every beat ("in the middle of the narrow night alley in @alley, the
  wet asphalt ..., the shopfronts and steam on the left, the vending machine on the right, neon
  signs ..., red lanterns ..."): the previs went wide to show it all, then drifted back in. The
  same plan with one sentence ("A medium shot of @kyo standing in @alley from her knees up; the
  camera holds") held a steady medium, with the same LoRA on (user's previs A/B).
- "Raps into the camera" as a hop's ending: on a one-take rap MV the camera moved forward at the
  end of hops 1 and 2, both ending on it (user-watched). "Into" reads as travel toward the lens.
  Write "her eyes on the lens" (rule 22).
- A recurring prop held by words alone: the gist held, the design changed every appearance.
- A run away from the camera without "her back to the camera": it played in reverse.
- A run toward a place, shot from in front: the place came out behind her.
- "From beside @b" with the two apart: @b drawn at the speaker's shoulder, twice.
- A masked face with no mouth state on a speaking hop: the mask "spoke". A masked face that nods
  in a close on a hop with lines: its mouth moved with speech.
- A person still in the scene left off a continuation: her look landed on her twin (lint
  `cast-missing`).
- A close whose eyeline named someone beside her, with no "alone in the frame": his head popped
  in (lint `pull-in`).
- "Toward the camera" in nearly every shot: far more lens looks than needed (lint `no-eye-target`).
- A story readable only with the plot in hand: a showcase opening with no want, an untriggered
  flashback, a cut from a yes to a new place.

- `sand_tie` dialogue with several lines stacked in one timed sentence that ended the window:
  the next window restated it and the lines were spoken again, several times in a one-minute
  take (lints `carry-line`, `stacked-lines`).

- An outfit phrase written only in a hop's `[Shot 1]`: the jacket was gone in the close after the
  cut (11-20). Every shot that shows the person carries the phrase (lint `state`).
- A prop taken off a hop whose camera sees where it rests: that hop ended on an empty chair, and the
  continuation after it drew the shirt popping onto the chair mid-walk, twice, whatever its words
  said (11-20). The END frame decides what the next hop starts with (continuity.md, START → END).

- A cut in the middle of one continuous action (a man walks in, cut, he leans on the counter): read as a
  jump. Keep the action in one shot and move the camera with it (11-15, user-watched).
- A vanish written as a cut back to the same framing with the person gone: read as an error. Reveal the
  empty spot with a move (a close on a watcher, the camera arcing round until the spot comes into view).
- A shot that leaves out someone sitting in the frame: at the next cut he popped in with a prop that had not
  been there, and an unnamed cup vanished (11-15). Name everyone and every prop from the shot's first frame.
- A tilt down to someone's feet from a camera behind a counter (11-15, three renders and previews): the
  counter hides the floor beyond it, so the model removed the counter and the room floated. Ask what the
  camera can see from where it stands; show it with a low angle from the room side or by people looking
  (lint `tilt-past-near`).
- A spot asking for contact the plate cannot give ("one elbow on the counter top" where the plate's low
  stools stand a step back from a bar-height counter): the stools stayed where the plate had them and the
  man was drawn at the counter, sitting on air (11-15). Look at the plate and write only the contact
  its furniture allows.
- Three speakers trading back-to-back lines in one wide (11-15, twice): the model gave each line to the
  clearest face, merged two lines into one in the wrong voice and rushed the third. The proven form is
  above (each line its own shot). A line written into a walk or an action stretch came out early, and a
  line given to a face looking down went to the biggest face in frame (lint `multi-speaker`).
- Six shots in a 14 s hop with lines (11-15): the model drew fewer shots than written and every line
  slid one shot early onto the wrong face (lint `shot-count`; 3-4 shots per hop with lines is proven).

## Unproven (from one failure; test before a piece depends on it)

- Camera placement over move variety (camera.md, "Place the camera"). A three-person 15-hop
  story MV used seven move families but sat mostly at medium and eye level, framed from the
  front, slow or still (25% bold placements). The user read it as "zoom in, truck, zoom in ...
  too safe", a slideshow again (11-22). A one-singer MV with 56% bold placements (floor,
  body-mounted, airborne, whips landing on the beat) was rated the best so far. Not yet the same
  story both ways; `check_plan.py` prints the mix under "review by reading".

- A mask-on moment on screen (the man unmasked from behind, then pressing the mask over his face)
  ended the hop on a fully unmasked, invented face, and the next continuations would have kept it
  (11-12). Never write a masked character unmasked; start him masked.

- A `motion_context` restart (`anchor: "restart"`) on a stack with character sheets put the sheet
  itself into frame 0 (11-11). Two later MVs wrote every new place as a hard-cut continuation
  instead (`join: "hard_cut"`, no anchor, the hop before ending on the face) and both were rated
  the best so far; the checker still warns on that form, so waive it with this reason. Not yet a
  same-seed A/B against the restart.

- A masked character kept far in the background with no sheet on the hop, carried by words only
  ("a man in a black sweatshirt, a white skull half-mask over his mouth and jaw, his bare forehead,
  brows and eyes above it"), came out as a whole cartoon skull head every time; the same character
  with his sheet on the hop, close, looked right (11-13). For a recurring masked or costumed
  character, put the sheet on every hop where he appears, even small, and keep the words to
  clothes and place; the sheet carries the mask.

- `sand_tie` dialogue with one line per timed sentence and every window ending on a line-free
  action (the fix for the repeated lines above).

- Dialogue density of about two words a second (three or four capped turns per 10 s hop) without
  gibberish or swapped mouths. Written after a chain ran at 0.7 words a second and played thin.

- Two voice clips close in pitch (two low male voices) swapped timbre on lines even with each line
  alone in its own close (11-15). Keep the cast's voices apart (story_cast_plates.md).
- A dialogue hop rebuilt as four shots, one line each, the speaker alone and facing the camera
  (after six shots and a shared wide had failed): the user called it "still kinda iffy, wrong people
  speaking, but it worked out" (11-15). Better, not yet clean; the close voices above may be the rest.
- Three plates of one room made as separate generations: the room changed size and layout from angle
  to angle (11-15). One floor plan repeated in every plate prompt, or views edited from one master
  picture, are the candidates.

- `dialogue_lock` on a `motion_context` film: safe so far (lip sync and sound bed held), never yet
  seen removing babble. Does nothing under `hand_tie`.
- A prop sheet for a prop that changes state.
- A continuity sheet vetted before the beats, with spots as fixed phrases checked as `states`
  (in a two-person single-room piece, every blocking slip came from positions written differently
  from shot to shot).
- Off-frame eyeline side matching the person's side in the wide (a pull-back drew the person on
  the side the eyes pointed).
- A continuation written as one take (camera moves, no `[Shot N]` cuts) with the cuts in restarts.
- A restart opening on a different size or angle than the previous hop's end (same framing read
  as a jump cut).
- No footstep sound to close a shot after a step; every step with an end mark (an extra step back
  and forth appeared otherwise).
- A lone short line in a long window being said twice; fill the window with the line plus an
  action.
- The adult page's laws (rear torso lock, first-person POV, busy mouth, the anal map sentences
  repeated in every window): held in single-clip writing; untested across hops and joins.
- Life, energy and expression kept small to medium in chains, pose-locked at joins (carried over
  from single-clip writing, where large is the default).
- A reveal kept dry by naming the room sound across the cut.
- A high angle over sheeted people with no plate from that height (eye level twice).
- A view that turns round with every person given a side again.
- A score across a no-line hop in a busy place, against crowd speech.
- A background figure kept to a pose, against an unwritten insert.
- Cutting between two places inside one hop (the plates swapped once).
- Look-alike hair: one took the other's glasses on a hop without the owner. Cause unknown.
- A hand action on a prop written step by step.
- A masked character kept still on speaking hops, his reactions moved to no-line hops.
- Busy frames stay quiet: silent faces given business with its own sound.
- A chase covered from many camera places, screen direction fixed.
- A camera device into a memory, mirrored on the way back.
- A different reaction for every face, and blinks written.
- No body turn in a `hand_tie` one-take hop: "turning his shoulders toward the microphone" at
  2.0 s came with a cut there (the previs had also drifted closer; fixed in the node 10-28).
- Any hand task finished 2 s before a performer's first vocal: with faders worked until 1 s before
  the first line, the lip sync started a second or two late.
