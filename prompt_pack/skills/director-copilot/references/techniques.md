# Techniques: what each one is for, what it turns into, what to write

A working glossary of camera, framing, composition, lens, light, atmosphere, time and editing
techniques, for every kind of piece (film, MV, vlog, adult, sitcom). Read with [camera](camera.md)
for how a move is written and [comedy](comedy.md) for comic timing.

Every technique has a **cousin** the model draws when the words are loose, and a **tell** that
shows the miss on screen. The fix is always the same shape: write the one thing only the real
technique has. None of this page is render-proven in a chain unless [status](status.md) says so;
the section on [tested single-clip moves](#tested-single-clip-moves) held on single H3 clips.

## Camera movement

### Travel versus turning the lens

| Technique | Use it for | It turns into / the tell | Write |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push in (dolly in) | a thought landing, power arriving | a zoom: the frame grows, nothing near slides | real travel, one fixed lens, a near thing sliding out of frame, an end framing tied to a moment |
| Pull out (dolly out) | release, isolation, a reveal of the room | a zoom out | travel backward; name the things that come in at the edges, in order |
| Zoom (slow) | scrutiny, suspicion, a watcher | a push in | the camera stays put, only the lens tightens, background flattening; a hold on the end frame |
| Crash / rapid zoom | a shock, a sting, a punchline | a fast push in, or a creep that never lands | camera stays put, the lens snaps in a few frames, then dead still |
| Dolly zoom | vertigo, a world tilting under someone | a plain push or zoom | the camera travels one way while the lens goes the other; the face stays the same size, the background stretches or squeezes |
| Super dolly in / out | a face arriving too fast; a room bursting open | a slow push | a long path, violent foreground slide, the end frame named |
| Parallax / slider | depth, a small reveal | a pan | sideways travel with a named near object outrunning the far ones |

### Turning on the spot

| Technique | Use it for | Tell | Write |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pan | joining two things in one place | a sideways track (things shear) | the camera stays where it is and only turns; level horizon; composed start and end |
| Tilt up / down | height, a reveal above or below | a crane (the camera rises) | the camera stays at one height and only tips; the new top or bottom of the frame is the fact |
| Whip pan / whip tilt | energy, a hidden join, a snap to a reactor | a fast readable pan | a smear in the middle, a sharp dead stop on the target |
| Dutch angle / roll | unease, a world off true | a random 60° tilt on every shot | a fixed 20-30° roll, held; a dutch *roll* starts level and rolls over time to a chosen tilt |
| Camera roll | a world flipping | a tilted still frame | the view turns round the lens until up is no longer the top of the frame |

### Travelling with or round a person

| Technique | Use it for | Tell | Write |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arc / orbit | circling, sizing someone up, a reveal round a shoulder | a pan | a curve at a constant distance, lens on the person, a near thing sliding across; name the side and the stop (half a turn at most on a person) |
| 360° orbit | a statue moment, a frozen peak | the person turns instead | the camera travels a full circle while the person stays almost still, background wrapping |
| Tracking / trucking | walks, runs, energy | a pan that loses them | the camera travels alongside at their pace, size held, the street peeling past |
| Follow | going somewhere with them | a static wide | behind at a fixed distance, the head in the near frame, the space opening ahead |
| Leading (backing away) | arrival, owning the space | a static | the camera backs away ahead of them at their pace |
| Walk-and-talk | talk with momentum | a silent follow, a back of a head | two people walking and talking, camera leading or flanking, faces readable, the place changing |
| Hero cam | the lead walking toward a fight or stage | a medium at eye level | low (hip or knee), three-quarter, rim light, walking pace |
| Steadicam / gimbal | a long connected glide through rooms | handheld bounce | carried, smooth, horizon held, no footfall bounce |
| Handheld | panic, nearness, documentary | a shaking still frame | someone holding it: breath, small corrections, attention moving |
| SnorriCam | panic, drink, a body out of control | a follow | the camera fixed to the body facing the face; the face stays pinned while the room swings |
| Locked-on / car grip | speed, a body in a vehicle | the vehicle drifting in frame | the camera fixed to the car or body, metal or shoulder in the near frame, only the world streaming |
| Choreo move | dance, a fight, a gesture | a camera that keeps floating | the camera starts and stops on the same count as the body |

### Rising and falling

| Technique | Use it for | Tell | Write |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crane up / down | scale, an ending, an arrival | a tilt | the camera itself rises or sinks, a near object sliding, the person readable all the way |
| Pedestal up / down | a body reveal, a new eyeline | a tilt or an arc | straight up or down, the angle unchanged |
| Crane over the head | a fall into a map, a god's view | a high wide | the arm rises up and over until it looks nearly straight down |
| Jib | a small lift into an intimate frame | a crane | a short arm, a few feet of travel |
| Aerial / drone | geography, isolation | a still map | high, the person a findable speck; a pull back rises and recedes, a push in descends onto a readable person |
| FPV drone | rush, an impossible path | a smooth drone | first-person flight, banking, named obstacles, a physical end frame |

### Through things

| Technique | Use it for | Tell | Write |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pass-through / through object | a transition, a reveal, entering a world | a cut | the camera travels through the named gap (keyhole, window, glass) and keeps its shape on the way |
| Eyes in / mouth in | into a memory, an obsession | a stopped close-up | a slow push that ends with the iris (or the mouth) filling the frame |
| Flying transition | joining two places in one breath | a cut | fly through a named threshold and land somewhere readable |

## Framing and shot size

| Shot | What it is | Tell it missed | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme close-up | one feature: an eye, a mouth, a trigger | a close-up | name the feature and crop inside the face |
| Choker | hairline to chin | a close-up | emotion with nowhere to hide |
| Close-up | head and top of shoulders | any tight face | the default for an important line |
| Medium close-up | cut at the chest, a hand still possible | a medium | a slice of room stays |
| Medium | cut at the waist | every talking frame | body language plus face |
| Cowboy | cut mid-thigh, hands in, feet out | a seated medium | the gun hand, the stance |
| Full body | head to feet, person still big | a wide | the outfit, the stance, a dance |
| Wide / long | the place speaks, bodies small | an establishing postcard | the map the next cut uses |
| Extreme long | a person as a mark in the land | an empty landscape | keep them findable |
| Establishing | where and when, then coverage | a pretty shot that says nothing | shows the doors and routes the scene uses |
| Master | the whole scene's blocking in one frame | a wide too empty to play | every important body and path in |
| Two-shot | two people at a similar size, the gap visible | over-the-shoulder | the relationship is the gap |
| Three-shot | three principals in a triangle | a two-shot cropping the third | one of the three slightly out of line |
| Group | a small ensemble, faces readable | a crowd with no leader | one person owns the frame by place or light |
| Over-the-shoulder | one soft shoulder, one sharp face looking at it | a two-shot | the reverse matches it, the line held |
| Single | one person alone | a two-shot leaking in | say "alone in the frame" |
| Reaction | the face receiving | the face delivering (mouth moving) | eyes on the source, hold |
| Insert | an object in the action, at real scale | a pretty detail | name whose hands; same light as the scene |
| Cutaway | something else in the same now | an insert | when the cut returns, time has moved on |
| Cut-in | same axis, tighter on a fact already there | a new angle | a step closer, not a new view |
| Gesture | sized to the moving part | a body shot | let the action finish |
| Interview | eyes just off the lens, a listener implied | a lens look | stable talking-head size |

## Angles

- **Eye level is the person's eyes, not the camera operator's.** A seated person gets a seated
  camera; a standing camera on a seated person already looks down.
- **Low angle:** below the eyes, tilted up, eyes and mouth still readable, ceiling in. A low but
  level camera is something else (calm, domestic).
- **High angle:** about 30° down, the face still readable. A raised camera that stays level is
  just a high view, with no power in it.
- **Bird's-eye / overhead:** steeply or straight down, floor as a map. Watch extra arms in seated
  groups seen from above.
- **Worm's-eye:** on the ground looking straight up, verticals rushing in. **Ground level** is
  different: lens on the floor looking along it, grit sharp in front.
- **Hip level:** at the belt, through the hands (gunfights, card games).
- **Three-quarter:** about 45° round, both eyes visible, one ear. Say both the turn and the
  height; they are separate.
- **Profile:** turned until the far eye is gone, but with a real head, not a cut-out.
- **Point of view:** the owner never seen except hands or a tool; the height matches the person
  just shown looking. A mirror shows them: decide it.
- **Object POV / trunk shot:** the camera is the prop (inside the trunk, the fridge, the oven),
  its edge in frame and locked; faces lean in and look down.
- **Fourth wall:** eyes on the lens to address the viewer. A look just past the lens is not it.

## Composition

- **Look space:** the extra room goes on the side the face looks toward. Crushing it (the face at
  the edge it looks at) is for someone trapped, on purpose.
- **Headroom:** a little above the head. A face sitting low in big empty space is negative space,
  on purpose or not at all.
- **Rule of thirds:** eyes on a third line, the look into the larger field. **Centred:** dead
  centre, the two sides matched; for symmetry, put the camera on the room's axis.
- **Foreground interest / dirty frame:** a chosen object or shoulder soft at the edge, never over
  the mouth. **Clean frame:** nothing at the edges, said outright.
- **Frame in a frame:** a doorway, window or mirror bordering the person on at least three sides.
- **Layered depth:** a second story on another plane (someone in the doorway behind).
- **Leading lines:** an edge that actually runs to the person.
- **One-point perspective:** the camera on the axis of a corridor or road, both sides meeting at
  the centre.
- **Triangle:** three figures at three heights (one sits, one stands, one in a doorway).
- **Visual weight:** pick the winner and make the rest darker, softer, smaller or greyer.
- **Negative space:** a simple field round a small person, for loneliness or awe.
- **Tableau:** every body given a job, the hold is the coverage.
- **Reflections:** a surface that could see both, the bright side observed, the eyeline right.
- **Screen in a screen:** the inner screen's picture and words written out like a prop.

## Lenses

Focal length is camera distance. Say where the camera stands and the lens follows:

- **14-24 mm wide:** close to the person, room big around them; faces stretch only when the lens
  is very close.
- **35 mm:** a conversation distance, a face plus a true slice of the place.
- **50 mm:** a normal face at talking distance.
- **85 mm:** a portrait from further back, the background stacked and soft.
- **135-200 mm telephoto:** the camera far away, the background pressed flat against the person,
  crowds packed (a spy, a watcher, heat haze).
- **Fisheye:** a round warp around a clear centre (skate, club, a punch to the lens).
- **Macro:** a small thing filling the frame, texture lit like terrain.
- **Anamorphic:** oval out-of-focus lights and horizontal flare streaks; a wide frame alone is
  not anamorphic.
- **Split diopter:** a near face and a far face both sharp, one soft line between.
- **Tilt-shift:** a high view of a real place that looks like a model.
- **Shallow focus:** a real gap between planes and one eye sharp. **Deep focus:** near and far
  both sharp in one space. **Rack focus:** the framing holds while focus passes from one plane to
  the other.

## Light

Every light has a source in the story. Name it, and say which side of the face it falls on.

- **Key / fill / rim:** one main side, a softer opposite side, a thin edge from behind.
  **Low key:** one pool, the rest falls to dark. **High key:** soft, even, little shadow.
- **Hard light:** a small far source (sun, a bare bulb), crisp shadows. **Soft light:** a big
  source (a window, a wall it bounces off), gentle edges.
- **Side / split:** the key at 90°, half the face dark. **Rembrandt:** a small lit triangle under
  the far eye. **Butterfly:** the key high in front, a small shadow under the nose (glamour).
  **Loop:** a little off-centre. **Broad / short:** the lit side toward or away from the camera.
- **Top light:** straight above, eyes in shadow (interrogation, menace). **Under light:** from
  below the jaw (horror, a campfire).
- **Backlight / silhouette:** the bright source behind; for a silhouette, the body unlit and the
  limbs separated against the light.
- **Practicals:** lamps, neon, screens, candles in frame, and the face lit from them. **Neon:** the
  nearest tube is the key, mixed colour on the skin is fine. **Candle:** warm, fast falloff.
- **Motivated / window light:** everything agrees with the window or the lamp it comes from.
- **Golden hour:** low warm sun, long shadows. **Blue hour:** no sun, a cool sky still bright, the
  lamps on.
- **Gobo / dappled:** a hard beam through blinds, leaves or water.
- **Volumetric:** a beam visible in haze or smoke, the body breaking it.
- **Epiphany:** start darker and let a source find the face at the thought.
- **Eye light:** a small catchlight in the eyes.

## Atmosphere

Particles show only when **lit from behind**. Name the source, the species and that they hang in
the air:

- **Rain:** drops in the air, backlit, the ground wet. **Wet-down:** wet ground and reflections
  without rain in the air.
- **Fog / mist / haze:** sharp near, paler planes behind, lights blooming.
- **Smoke / steam:** a visible source (a cigarette, a cup, a vent) and the path it rises along.
- **Dust motes:** a hard beam through dark air, slow countable specks.
- **Snow:** big backlit flakes, white settled on surfaces, footprints.
- **Sparks / embers:** a hot source, short glowing trails, dark air.
- **Underwater:** the camera under, cloth floating, rippling light, silt.

## Time and motion

- **Slow motion:** the action still travels through, gravity holds; say the slowing (and the
  sound dropping) on the moment.
- **Speed ramp:** fast in, slowed at the peak, snapping back to speed.
- **Freeze frame:** camera and world both stop on one frame; in the edit. **Frozen in motion /
  bullet time:** the world hangs (debris, drops, hair) while the camera keeps moving round it.
- **Fast motion / time-lapse:** hours in seconds, a still camera, shadows travelling. **Human
  time-lapse:** one person holding still while the crowd races.
- **Reverse motion:** one action played backward (liquid rising into a glass); edit or render.
- **Stutter / step printing:** a broken, counted cadence.
- **Long take / oner:** one route through connected actions with a person anchoring it. A static
  talk does not need one.
- **Motion blur:** streaking lights and limbs. **Low shutter:** the opposite, crisp staccato.
- **Boomerang / loop:** forward then back, or the last frame matching the first.

## Editing and transitions

Most of these happen in the edit; the render's job is to give the cut its two sides.

- **Match on action:** cut during the move, same hand, same speed, same direction. In H3 a
  continuous action is safer as one take (status.md); keep this for restarts.
- **Match cut / graphic match:** the same shape in the same place across two different things.
  **Match motion:** the same direction and speed across a jump in place.
- **Smash / crash cut:** a committed state, then a hard cut to its opposite. Change two things
  (sound and size, or tone and time).
- **Jump cut:** the same framing, time removed. Keep the framing almost identical or nothing
  jumps.
- **Axial cut:** two or three hard cuts closer along the same line, the pose locked.
- **J-cut / L-cut (pre-lap):** the next sound starts before the picture changes, or the first
  sound runs on after it.
- **Cross-cutting:** two places at once; both need their own plates and hops.
- **Flash cut:** a handful of frames of another picture, then back.
- **Montage:** distinct images in order, the join doing the telling.
- **Invisible cut:** hidden in a whip, darkness or a body crossing the lens.
- **Dissolve / fade / wipe / iris / split screen:** edit tools (scripts/transitions.py for dips).
- **Set transition:** the body continues while the walls change round it; hard in H3.

## Effects worth knowing

- **Double exposure:** two images at once, one a ghost. **Echo:** a fainter offset copy of the
  same pose.
- **Duplication / twins:** one face twice in one frame, shadows shared.
- **Forced perspective / scale shift:** the same room at a new body-to-room size.
- **Levitation / floating:** a visible gap under the feet, cloth hanging down.
- **Morph / transformation:** matched pose and light, features travelling, landing on one form.
- **Night vision / thermal / x-ray / infrared:** say the device and the palette.
- **Lens flare / halation / light leak / grain / chromatic edges:** tie each to a real source or
  the film stock.
- **Typography:** the exact words, the surface they are on.
- **Cinemagraph:** one moving element in a still frame.

## Tested single-clip moves

These held on single H3 clips (not chains). In a chain, treat them as one take inside one hop,
and keep the join windows calm around them.

- **Snap and freeze:** any crash zoom, whip or impact jolt snaps fast and then holds dead still.
- **Start and stop on the timestamp:** "At 00:02.000-00:05.000, the camera starts a slow arc
  round her left side and stops on her face." A hold is "the camera holds dead still on ...".
- **One axis per beat.** Never push, orbit, tilt and zoom at once.
- **Pace a scan across the beats.** A rise from the feet to the face (or a descent) is split
  across the beats: the feet only in the first, the middle held, the face in the last. Written
  in one beat, it jumps to the face at once.
- **A continuous clip starts where the last ended;** a cut clip opens on a contrasting size or
  angle.
- **Don't default to push-ins.** Writers spam them; reach for holds, trucks, whips, zooms, arcs,
  pull-backs and focus pulls first.
- **Doorframe slider reveal:** a dark, soft doorframe or pillar fills two-thirds of the frame, a
  sliver of the person beyond; the camera trucks sideways until the edge clears and they are in
  full view.
- **Occlusion wipe:** a near shoulder, curtain or pillar sweeps across the lens to hide a big
  change of angle inside one take; pair it with a speed contrast (a statue-still face, then the
  camera whipping away).
- **Floor-planted camera:** the camera lies flat on an empty floor looking up; say the floor is
  bare (no phone, no case), or a phone appears.
- **Time-freeze orbit:** open on the peak of a move suspended in the air (a leap, hair, drops or
  sparks hanging), everything perfectly still while the camera whips round it.
- **Macro eye to wide:** open on one eye filling the frame and pull rapidly back to the person
  in the place.
- **Selfie camera:** say "exactly two arms": the camera arm reaching to a named corner of the
  frame, the free arm on the other side, and no phone or stick in view. A selfie moved round
  the face (orbit, whip down to the feet, a pass behind the neck) keeps that arm in every beat.
- **Hands on the lens:** a palm pressing the glass shoves the view sideways; a finger hooked on
  the glass drags it back.
- **Fight camera:** a low full-body hero frame; the camera whips in the direction of each strike
  so it feels them, then orbits a quarter turn on a charge.
- **Light first, effects at the midpoint:** show the clean light on skin, hair and clothes for
  the first half (identity locks on it), then bring in the effect (strobe, fog, sparks,
  confetti) from the middle.
- **Power-up hair:** with an aura or lightning, the hair lifts and floats as if underwater but
  keeps its own colour and shape from the sheet; otherwise it turns into cartoon spikes.
- **Smoke exhale:** a slow draw with the tip glowing, then a dense plume blown toward the lens,
  curling, with light cutting through it.
- **A pour:** one hand tips the bottle, the other steadies the glass; the stream hits the ice,
  swirls, the level rises, then the glass lifts, with the clink.
- **Groups dancing:** give each dancer slightly different timing, head tilt or step; identical
  unison looks robotic.
- **Impacts need a response:** a slam, spill, grab or break names the contact and what it does
  to the thing hit.
- **Mirror POV:** the view looks straight into the mirror at eye level, hands low in the frame or
  visible in the reflection; say what the reflection shows in positive words.
- **Sound words in dialogue get spoken:** "tsk tsk" or "gulp" inside `<d>` are read out as words;
  write the sound as the voice would make it ("Tch.") or move it out of the line.
