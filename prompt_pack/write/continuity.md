Before any beat exists, work out the blocking for the whole chain, the way a director and a script supervisor would, and put it in `continuity`:
- `set_layout`: read every place still (and start image, if attached) as a floor plan in screen terms: near->far depth order, left/right landmarks, which way furniture faces, open floor vs wall-flush, and missing angles.
- `spots`: every place a person stands or sits, each as ONE fixed phrase giving (1) screen quadrant/side and depth side relative to the camera or furniture (e.g. 'on the bottom-left foreground of the counter, her back three-quarter to bottom-left of shot, facing @b in top-right' or 'on the near side of the low table, between the table and the camera, at frame right'), (2) body facing, and (3) eyeline target. Repeat it word for word from that camera. Never use vague phrases like 'across the table' or 'by the window'. Outfit phrases name the garment's defining cut (neckline, length).
- `hops`: one row per hop: `hop`, `start` (spot, screen side, facing, posture, props held), `action` (what moves on screen), `end` (exact final spots, facing, posture, props), `end_framing`. Every `join: continuous` hop opens from the previous hop's exact `end`. Before a `motion_context` continuation, end on a face at medium or closer with hands at rest, and avoid `[Shot 2]` cuts in its first 2 seconds.
- `cuts`: every `anchor: 'restart'` (paired with `join: 'hard_cut'`) and every `[Shot N]` cut inside a hop: `at`, `why` (a look, line, sound, or action needing another angle), and `matches`. Sides belong to the camera: whenever a cut or restart changes camera angle, re-state EVERY visible subject's new screen quadrant, depth, facing, and eyeline for THAT camera. A restart opens on a clearly different size or angle than the previous end and on an action already starting.
- Screen direction & barriers: walks away from camera explicitly say 'back to the camera'; walks toward camera/someone name where they stop; keep exit sides consistent across cuts; name both sides of barriers (glass, railings, gates); add 'alone in the frame' in close-ups looking off-frame; omit `directives.camera` & `directives.framing` when directing moves or [Shot N] cuts directly in the beat.
- `weak_spots`: any angle the pictures leave out, and the fixed background phrase you will name for it every time.
