This is a MUSIC VIDEO cut to the song, which plays under every hop and drives every mouth. These rules replace the spoken-word targets of the length table. Each is a finding from real renders, with its reason:
1. Start from an idea, not a list of places. Give the video one idea beyond the lyrics (a device, a game, a rule of its world), one visual hook that belongs to this video only and comes back bigger each time the main refrain returns, and what grows from section to section. Put the idea and the hook in `one_line` and the growth in `sections`. Without an idea the hops read as a reel of places with someone walking and doing random things in front of each.
2. No <d> lines and no (S1) speaker tags. Say who sings or raps and how ("@x raps fast, eyes on the lens"); the song supplies the words. Write "eyes on the lens", not "into the camera": "into" reads as travel toward the lens and pulls the shot in. Give the singing to chosen shots only; the same to-lens singing clause on every shot plays as cut, sing, cut, sing. Shots built around a move or an effect carry no mouth line.
3. Everyone else reacts with their body -- points, laughs, daps, throws an arm round a shoulder -- and the whole crew shouts the hooks together. Nobody stands posed. Someone alone on screen over vocals mouths the song, so give non-singers their solo shots on instrumental bars.
4. Direct every move. Give the performer a new named move every bar or two, each with an end ("snaps her head left on the downbeat and freezes"); one gesture written for a whole window loops. Named pose hits that land on the downbeat and hold read as choreography; "dances" reads as flapping arms. Dance is the whole body, an action is one part: a dance move says what the feet, hips, torso, arms and head do together on the count, even a simple step-touch ("steps right and touches her left foot in, hips swaying with it, shoulders rolling, head tilting on the touch"); hands and face alone (leaning in, slicing the air, nodding at the lens) read as rapping with gestures. Inside one continuous take use at most two timed windows, each at least about 1.7 s, on the key moment: many or short windows render as stutter cuts. Cuts between shots ([Shot N]) are free.
5. Energy comes from cuts and from where the camera is. High-energy hops hold 4-7 [Shot N] cuts that change angle and size each time; verses fewer. Place the camera, not only move it: on the floor looking up, fixed to the performer's body, high above, behind something in the foreground, whips that land dead still on the beat. A hop of medium shots at eye level from the front reads as a slideshow whatever the moves are. Match energy to the section: `intro` builds atmosphere; `verse` follows the lead's rhythm and movement; `chorus` and `drop` hit peak physical energy with the boldest camera; `bridge` / `breakdown` shifts framing or pace; `outro` lands and settles.
6. Give every hop, from hop 1, one visible effect in the frame that lands on a named move or beat (light, particles, weather, the world reacting to her). Effects that start only mid-song leave the opening plain.
7. Every hop has an open, a change and a definite end, and uses the top of its word budget on action: a thin beat finishes early and idles. Describe what the mouth and body do, never the song's structure ("sings the chorus", "the last line"): the model has no map of the song.
8. `R` hops are bar-aligned cuts (`join: hard_cut` or `match_cut` on hop 2+); `C` hops continue the shot before them (`join: continuous`).
9. The overall_soundscape is the song itself: name the music and the room (the bass, the crowd), never a voice saying words.
